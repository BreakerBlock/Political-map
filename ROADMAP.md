# BharatYatra roadmap

## Priority backlog (from Utkarsh, 2026-10-25) — current focus
More to see, and more excitement, ahead of further trip-planner features.
- [ ] **Data-rich map** *(in progress — 289 → 329 → 368 → 415 places after run 3)* — grow well beyond the original 289 places, state by state and category by category (thinnest first): ~25–40 real, well-known places per run with verified coordinates, each checked against `india-boundary.geojson`; deepen existing entries (better one-liners, best time to visit, Wikipedia photo titles).
- [ ] **Highlights & must-do slides** *(in progress — viewer, Highlights of India and a must-do deck for all 8 categories shipped 2026-10-28; 10 more slides 2026-10-29 (86 total); per-state highlights next)* — a full-bleed, swipeable story/carousel (touch swipe, arrow keys, buttons) for "Highlights of India" and "Must-do experiences" in each category (e.g. rafting at Rishikesh, paragliding at Bir Billing, street food in Chandni Chowk, Hornbill Festival, tigers at Ranthambore). Big photo, punchy one-liner, state, best season, **Show on map** and **+ Add to trip**. Entry points: header button, a "Must-do" link per category, an optional first-visit teaser. Slices: viewer + Highlights of India → must-do per category → per-state highlights.
- [ ] **"Wow" moments** — richer landing, "Surprise me", seasonal picks ("Best in December").

Hovering a stop in the timeline lifts and pulses its pin and brings it to the front. On desktop, opening **My trip** folds the category grid, state picker and search into one line (Categories + coloured dots for the active categories + a **Filters ▾** button to peek), so a 5-stop trip and its actions fit without scrolling at 1366×800. *(2026-10-21)*

The grip also works from the keyboard with ↑/↓. Phones show grip + ✕ only; desktop keeps the ↑/↓ buttons. *(2026-10-20)*
- [x] **Visual refresh, pass 3 — trip planner & sidebar** — the trip panel is now a timeline: a summary card (stops, road km, drive time, straight-line km for sea legs) over a vertical line joining numbered stops in category colours, with each leg as a chip (car icon for road legs on a solid line; dashed line and plane icon for sea/air legs; spinner while routing). Trip stop names are now HTML-escaped. *(2026-10-19)*

## Done after the backlog
- [x] **Day-by-day trip plan** — the My trip timeline now splits the route into driving days (Day 1, Day 2 … headers with each day's drive time and km), marks overnight stops with a 🌙 Night N tag, alternates the route colour per day on the map, and adds a "days" count to the trip summary. A **Max drive / day** switch (4 h / 6 h / 8 h, remembered on the device) re-plans instantly. Sea/air legs to the islands become their own travel day; a single leg longer than the limit is flagged as a long day; days that still use straight-line estimates are marked ≈. Email itineraries include the day headings. Fix: trips restored from a shared link or the browser now show their numbered stop pins immediately (they used to appear only after the map re-rendered). *(2026-10-22)*
- [x] **Islands on the map & pins checked against the boundary** — every place (289) was tested against `india-boundary.geojson`. The outline had no Lakshadweep at all (Agatti's pin floated in open sea) and was missing Pamban island (Rameswaram), Barren Island, Narcondam and a few other outer Andaman & Nicobar islands, and two Sundarbans islands; 19 island outlines from Natural Earth 1:10m land were added (islands only — the mainland and the Kashmir boundary are untouched; the mist mask and halo pick them up). Added LAKSHADWEEP and ANDAMAN & NICOBAR map labels. Fixed three pins that sat outside India: **Sundarbans** (was in Bangladesh → 21.84, 88.89, the national park), **Singalila NP** (was in Nepal → 27.12, 88.07) and **Dawki River** (on the border line → 25.19, 92.02). Remaining near-misses are ≤2 km and come from the simplified coastline. *(2026-10-23)*
- [x] **Map labels that never hide under pins** — city and state names now sit in their own layer above the pins and are decluttered on every zoom/pan: a name never overlaps a pin, a cluster, a map control or another name. City names try right, left, above and below their dot; when a city sits under a cluster, its dot hides and its name is set just beside the cluster (e.g. "Mumbai ④"). Priority: the six metros, then states, then other big cities, then smaller ones; state names nudge a little to find room. Phones now show **New Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad on the opening all-India view** (before: no names at all), and desktop no longer has labels like Kolkata, Chennai or Amritsar buried under cluster bubbles. Labels fade during zoom and turn white-on-dark over satellite imagery. *(2026-10-24)*
